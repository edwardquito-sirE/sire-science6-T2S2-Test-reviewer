Competency 1

Ecosystem Interactions: Helpful vs. Harmful Effects

Explain how interactions between living things (biotic) and non-living things (abiotic) bring good or harm.

🐝🌱

A. Living-to-Living Interactions (Biotic vs. Biotic)

👍 Helpful Interactions (Good)
  • 1. Mutualism (+/+) Both organisms benefit! Example: Honeybees and Sunflowers. Bees gather sweet nectar for food, while carrying pollen on their fuzzy bodies to help flowers reproduce.
  • 2. Commensalism (+/0) One organism benefits while the other is unharmed. Example: Orchids growing on high tree trunks. The orchid gets closer to sunlight without absorbing food or water from the tree.
  • 3. Balanced Predation (Biological Control) Predators control pest populations. Example: Frogs preying on locusts/grasshoppers in rice fields prevents swarms from consuming human food crops.
👎 Harmful Interactions (Harm)
  • 1. Parasitism (+/-) The parasite benefits while the host is harmed. Example: Mosquitoes biting humans for blood, causing itching and transmitting dengue/malaria viruses. Also lice in scalp hair.
  • 2. Resource Competition (-/-) Organisms struggle for limited food, water, or territory. Example: Weeds competing with corn crops for soil nitrogen and sunlight, causing crop yield to drop.

B. Living-to-Non-Living Interactions (Biotic vs. Abiotic)

Helpful Abiotic Conditions

Sunlight & Air (CO₂): Essential for green plants to undergo photosynthesis and manufacture food (glucose) and produce oxygen for animals.

Water: Dissolves minerals in soil so plant roots can absorb them and transport fluid inside living tissues.

Loam Soil & Worms: Rich dark soil containing decomposed organic matter (humus) gives sturdy physical support and nutrients for root growth.

Harmful Extreme Abiotic Conditions

Severe Drought & Excessive Heat: Causes soil water to evaporate completely, causing plant wilting, leaf scorching, and root dehydration.

Flooding & Soil Waterlogging: Fills all soil air spaces with water, depriving plant roots of vital oxygen gas, leading to root rot and fungal decay.

Competency 2

Everyday Simple Machines at Home, School & Community

Observe and describe real-life examples and practical uses of the 6 simple machines around us.

🛠️🏡
📐 Slanted Surface

1. Inclined Plane

A flat, sloping surface connecting a lower elevation to a higher elevation.

🏡 Home: Staircase, doorstep ramp.

🏫 School: Playground slide, wheelchair access ramp.

🏙️ Community: Delivery truck loading ramp, highway off-ramp.

🪓 Cutting & Splitting

2. Wedge

Two inclined planes joined back-to-back ending in a sharp edge; cuts or splits objects.

🏡 Home: Kitchen knife, vegetable peeler, doorstop.

🏫 School: Push pins, paper cutter blade, pencil sharpener blade.

🏙️ Community: Firefighter axe, chisel, nail, snowplow blade.

⚖️ Pivoting Bar

3. Lever

A rigid bar or rod that rests and pivots on a fixed point called a fulcrum.

🏡 Home: Bottle opener, broom, tweezers, claw hammer.

🏫 School: Seesaw, stapler, paper hole puncher, scissors.

🏙️ Community: Crowbar at construction sites, wheelbarrow.

🏗️ Grooved Wheel & Rope

4. Pulley

A wheel with a grooved rim and a rope/cable wrapped around it to lift heavy loads.

🏡 Home: Window blinds pull cord, clothesline pulley.

🏫 School: School flagpole mechanism, stage curtain pull.

🏙️ Community: Construction tower crane, deep water well bucket.

🎡 Rotating Shaft

5. Wheel and Axle

A large circular wheel secured to a smaller central shaft; turning one turns the other.

🏡 Home: Doorknob, rolling pin, water faucet handle.

🏫 School: Pencil sharpener crank handle, office chair wheels.

🏙️ Community: Car steering wheel, bicycle wheels, Ferris wheel.

🔩 Spiral Threads

6. Screw

An inclined plane wrapped spirally around a central cylinder or shaft.

🏡 Home: Threaded jar lids, wood screws, light bulb base.

🏫 School: Height-adjustable desk chairs, globe stand screw.

🏙️ Community: Car jack screw, water well auger drill.

Competency 3

Guided Investigation: Advantages & Limitations

Demonstrate how simple machines trade distance for effort force and explore their trade-offs.

🧪🔬

Golden Rule of Simple Machines (Work Conservation)

Simple machines DO NOT reduce the total amount of Work required ($\text{Work} = \text{Force} \times \text{Distance}$). They make work easier by multiplying your effort force in exchange for pushing/pulling over a longer distance!

Inclined Plane Investigation

Advantage: Requires much LESS effort force to push a heavy box up a ramp than lifting it straight up vertically!

Limitation / Trade-off: You must move the box over a much LONGER distance along the slanted ramp surface.

Mechanical Advantage (MA) = Length of Ramp ÷ Height of Ramp

Wedge Investigation

Advantage: Concentrates applied downward pushing force into powerful sideways splitting force to cut tough materials easily.

Limitation / Trade-off: High surface friction causes edges to become dull over time, requiring sharpening and strong initial push.

Investigation of the 3 Classes of Levers

Class 1 Lever
Fulcrum in MIDDLE

Examples: Seesaw, crowbar, scissors.

Advantage: Changes force direction (push down to lift load up) and multiplies force if fulcrum is close to load.

Class 2 Lever
Load in MIDDLE

Examples: Wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener.

Advantage: Always multiplies effort force ($MA > 1$). You exert less force than the weight of the heavy load!

Class 3 Lever
Effort in MIDDLE

Examples: Broom, tweezers, fishing rod, human forearm.

Advantage: Increases speed and distance moved by the load! Gives reach and precision (even though $MA < 1$).

Investigation of Pulley Systems

1. Fixed Pulley

MA = 1 (Flagpole, window blinds)

Advantage: Changes force direction. Allows you to pull downward with your body weight to lift objects upward.

2. Movable Pulley

MA = 2 (Attaches directly to load)

Advantage: Halves the required effort force ($100\text{ N load} = 50\text{ N effort}$ force needed)!

3. Block and Tackle

MA = Number of ropes (Crane pulley)

Advantage: Multiplies effort force significantly to lift heavy machinery easily, but requires pulling a very long rope length.
