Competency 1: Ecosystem Interactions (Good & Harm)

Items 1–10 in Mock Test

1. Helpful Living-to-Living Interactions

Organisms interact in nature to gain food, shelter, or reproduction support.

Mutualism (+/+): Honeybees gather nectar from flowers while helping flowers pollinate. Both benefit!
Commensalism (+/0): Orchids grow on high hardwood tree branches to reach sunlight. The orchid gets light while the tree is unharmed.
Balanced Predation: Frogs prey on grasshoppers, keeping insect pest populations under control so crops are protected.

2. Harmful Living-to-Living Interactions

Some biological relationships cause disease, structural injury, or growth stuntedness.

Parasitism (+/-): Mosquitoes suck blood from humans, causing skin itching and transmitting illnesses like dengue fever.
Resource Competition: Weeds growing in a cornfield compete with corn crops for limited soil minerals, water, and sunlight.

3. Helpful Living-to-Nonliving Interactions

Living organisms depend completely on non-living physical components for survival.

Sunlight & Plants: Green plants use light energy, water, and carbon dioxide to make food through Photosynthesis.
Soil & Nutrients: Rich loam soil provides physical root support and minerals for growing trees.

4. Harmful Living-to-Nonliving Conditions

Extreme environmental non-living conditions can harm or destroy living organisms.

Drought & Heat: Prolonged lack of rain (water scarcity) and high temperatures cause garden plants to wilt and die.
Flooding: Waterlogged soil starves plant roots of air oxygen, causing root rot.

Competency 2: Simple Machines Everywhere

Items 11–20 in Mock Test

1. Inclined Plane

A slanted flat surface that connects a lower elevation to a higher elevation.

Examples: Wheelchair ramps at school, playground slides, loading ramps on trucks, stairs.

2. Wedge

Two inclined planes placed back-to-back, ending in a sharp thin edge used to split or cut objects.

Examples: Kitchen knives, axes, scissors blades, doorstops, push pins, nails.

3. Lever

A rigid bar or board that pivots around a fixed point called a Fulcrum.

Examples: Playground see-saws, bottle openers, crowbars, wheelbarrows, tweezers, brooms.

4. Pulley

A grooved wheel holding a rope, chain, or cable used to lift heavy loads upward.

Examples: Flagpole pulleys at school, window blinds, water well buckets, construction cranes.

5. Wheel & Axle

A larger round wheel attached to a smaller central shaft or rod (axle) that rotate together.

Examples: Classroom doorknobs, bicycle wheels, car steering wheels, rolling pins.

6. Screw

An inclined plane wrapped spirally around a central shaft or cylinder (threads).

Examples: Wood screws, jar lids, light bulb bases, swivel chairs, bottle caps.

Competency 3: Advantages & Limitations of Simple Machines

Items 21–30 in Mock Test

1. Inclined Plane Investigation

Golden Rule: Work = Force × Distance ($W = F \times d$).

Advantage: Reduces the required pushing/pulling effort force to raise a heavy load. A longer, gentler slope makes lifting easier!
Limitation: You must move the load over a significantly longer distance compared to lifting it straight up.

2. Wedge Investigation

Converts a single forward pushing force into two perpendicular outward forces.

Advantage: Concentrates force to split logs, cut food, or hold doors tightly in place.
Limitation: Requires high friction to stay wedged; cutting edges become dull over time and require re-sharpening.

3. Lever Classes Investigation

Performance depends on the relative positions of Fulcrum, Load, and Effort.

Class 1 (Fulcrum in Middle - Crowbar, Scissors): Changes direction of force and multiplies force.
Class 2 (Load in Middle - Wheelbarrow): Always multiplies force (effort force is less than load force).
Class 3 (Effort in Middle - Sweeping Broom, Tweezers): Requires MORE effort force, but increases speed and reach!

4. Pulley Systems Investigation

Compares Fixed Pulleys vs Movable Pulleys vs Block & Tackle.

Fixed Pulley (Flagpole): MA = 1. Changes direction (pull down to lift up), but requires effort equal to load weight.
Movable Pulley: MA = 2. Cuts required effort force in half!
Block & Tackle Limitation: Multiple pulleys require pulling a huge length of rope, and friction reduces efficiency.
